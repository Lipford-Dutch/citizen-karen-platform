## Research & Innovation Proposal — Civic Complaint Orchestration Platform  

**Applicant Institution:** Karing USA / Citizen Karen  
**Project Title:** “Unified, Modular Orchestration of Government Complaint Submissions — Reducing Friction & Improving Civic Access”  
**Funding Requested:** $350,000  
**Project Duration:** 24 months  

### 1. Abstract / Summary  
Citizen Karen proposes to build and evaluate a novel platform that abstracts government agency complaint submission into a unified interface: modular “agency drivers,” dynamic form generation, asynchronous submission engine, audit‑grade data store, and user‑centric workflow. The project will pilot with one agency (FCC) and collect quantitative and qualitative data on usage, access equity, usability, compliance, and systemic impact.  

### 2. Background & Significance  
Government services and complaint portals are notoriously fragmented. This fragmentation creates inequity — low digital‑literacy, busy or underserved users get excluded, and agencies struggle with inconsistent, untraceable submissions. There is no standardized, scalable layer that sits between citizens and agencies. Citizen Karen aims to fill this gap, combining software engineering, civic‑tech, and public-interest infrastructure.  

This project aligns with civic‑innovation and public‑interest technology priorities: reducing bureaucratic friction, enhancing transparency, improving access.  

### 3. Research Questions & Objectives  

- **RQ1:** Does a unified intake portal increase submission rates and accessibility for under-served populations (low‑income, limited internet access, non‑native English)?  
- **RQ2:** Does modular agency‑driver architecture improve maintainability and onboarding speed of new agency integrations?  
- **RQ3:** What are the performance and reliability metrics (latency, error rates, success rate) of such an orchestration system at scale?  
- **Objective A:** Build core platform + FCC module + first pilot.  
- **Objective B:** User research and accessibility evaluation.  
- **Objective C:** Develop metrics, monitoring, audit & compliance framework.  
- **Objective D:** Publish findings; produce a public whitepaper & open‑source release.  

### 4. Methodology & Work Plan  

| Phase | Activities |
|-------|------------|
| Months 0–3 | System design, architecture, security & compliance setup, build core engine + data model |
| Months 4–6 | Develop FCC module & frontend (multi‑lingual, accessible), deploy pilot environment, begin user onboarding outreach |
| Months 7–12 | Collect pilot data (submissions, demographics, feedback), monitor performance, log & trace data, run audits & accessibility testing |
| Months 13–18 | Analyze data: usage patterns, accessibility, equity; refine platform; add instrumentation; prepare expansion plan |
| Months 19–24 | Prepare open‑source release; compile whitepaper/report; outreach to stakeholders (agencies, NGOs); plan next‑phase funding |

### 5. Expected Outcomes & Contributions  

- Publicly‑available, open‑source platform for unified gov complaint intake  
- Empirical data on civic‑tech usability, equity, accessibility, and impact  
- Academic / policy whitepaper on civic complaint portals, accessibility, public‑interest infrastructure  
- Foundation for large‑scale deployment, NGO adoption, or governmental partnership
